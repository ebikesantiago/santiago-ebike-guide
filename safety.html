Safety & gear

Minnesota trails are friendly — weather, bugs, and batteries less so. Pack like a slightly paranoid optimist.

The Great Northern is paved and mostly gentle, but multi-use trails mean mixed speeds, kids, dogs, and the occasional deer opinion. Stay sharp; stay kind.

Core habits

⛑️

Helmet + fit

Wear one that sits level and snug. E-bikes add speed quickly — your noggin is not a spare part.

🚦

20 mph mindset

County trail limit exists for a reason. Assist is a helper, not a personality.

👁️

See & be seen

Daytime running lights help; dusk lights are non-negotiable. Bright layers on cloudy prairie days.

🔊

Pass politely

Call out, slow down, give space. Assume earbuds and excited dogs exist.

Gear checklist

  • Helmet
  • Front white light + rear red light
  • Water bottles / hydration pack
  • Snacks
  • Spare tube, patches, tire levers
  • Mini-pump or inflator
  • Multi-tool
  • Phone + portable battery
  • E-bike charger (full-day rides)
  • Lock (town stops)
  • Bug spray (late spring–early fall)
  • Sunscreen + sunglasses
  • Light rain/wind layer
  • Gloves (cool mornings)
  • First-aid basics
  • ID / emergency contact info

Minnesota-specific curveballs

🦟 Bugs

Wetlands mean mosquitoes with ambition. Repellent, long sleeves at dusk, and don’t linger in still air without a plan.

🌡️ Heat & wind

Open stretches near farmland can bake or blow. Lower assist into headwinds; save battery for the way back.

🦌 Wildlife

Deer don’t check trail etiquette. Scan ahead at dawn/dusk; never chase critters for photos.

❄️ Winter

Ice = different sport. Fat tires help only if skill and legality match. When unsure, postpone.

Battery & mechanical basics

  • Start rides above ~60–70% charge if you’re doing a full GNRT out-and-back in wind/cold
  • Cold weather shrinks range — plan shorter loops
  • Know how to shift to a lower gear before stopping (easier restarts)
  • Check tire pressure before every ride — e-bikes are heavier
  • If something electronic feels weird, stop; don’t “see if it clears up at 20 mph”
Emergency note: Know roughly where you are (trailhead name / mile vibe). Cell coverage is usually fine near towns but can get spotty in pockets of refuge/wetland country.
