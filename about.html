About / FAQ / fun facts

A playful unofficial guide for people who want to e-bike the Santiago–Sherburne corner of Minnesota without corporate brochure energy.

Why this site exists

Santiago Township (Sherburne County) sits near growing trail connections, a national wildlife refuge, and towns with actual coffee. We wanted one place that is:

  • Honest about paved vs natural surfaces
  • Clear on the county’s 20 mph trail speed approach
  • Useful for half-day and full-day planning from the Santiago area
  • Willing to tell dad jokes on purpose

This is not an official Sherburne County or USFWS website. Rules change — verify before you ride.

FAQ

Can I ride my e-bike on Sherburne County trails?

Electric-assisted bicycles (as defined in Minnesota state law / county ordinance alignment) are allowed on county trails, including the Great Northern Regional Trail. Keep speeds at or under 20 mph.

Why isn’t the rule based on e-bike class?

County discussion noted that classes are hard to distinguish visually, making class-based rules tough to enforce. A universal speed limit is clearer for everyone.

Are ATVs or golf carts allowed on the trails?

No. Motorized vehicles such as ATVs, golf carts, and similar machines are not allowed on county trails.

Is Oak Savanna Park good for e-bikes?

It’s wonderful parkland, but surfaces lean crushed limestone / natural — better as a hike or careful check-before-you-ride situation. For a dedicated e-bike spin, prioritize the paved GNRT.

Can I e-bike in Sherburne National Wildlife Refuge?

Maybe on some roads — maybe not on trails. Refuge rules are separate from county trail rules. Check USFWS guidance before you go; many visitors have a better wildlife day on foot.

Where should I park?

Use designated GNRT parking locations (county lists multiple). Popular references include Zimmerman City Park / Lion’s Park and Elk River–area lots including Woodland Trails Regional Park. Confirm the current map on the county site.

What’s the landscape like around Santiago?

Flat-to-gently-rolling prairie, oak, and wetland country — roughly 45.53°N, 93.82°W — near Becker, Zimmerman, and Elk River.

Is this site affiliated with the county?

Nope. Friendly fan guide. Official info lives with Sherburne County Parks & Recreation and USFWS.
