Trip planner

Sample half-day and full-day adventures from the Santiago area — mix pavement miles with prairie wildlife (on foot when needed).

Santiago Township is a handy hub: drive a short hop to a Great Northern trailhead, ride out-and-back on smooth pavement, then reward yourself in Elk River, Zimmerman, or Becker.

Half-day · ~3–5 hours

Pavement spin + town treat

Best for: first visit, families, anyone who wants reliable e-bike surface without logistics drama.

  1. Launch from Santiago area — drive to a designated GNRT trailhead: Zimmerman Lion’s / City Park end or Elk River (Elk Lake Rd / Woodland Trails / southern lots). Confirm parking on the county map.
  2. Ride out-and-back on pavement — aim for 8–17 miles total depending on energy. Keep it under 20 mph, announce passes, soak up wetlands and woods.
  3. Stretch stop — Woodland Trails Regional Park is a nice midpoint vibe if you started from either end.
  4. Coffee / food in town — Elk River, Zimmerman, or Becker (see Stops for verified café ideas). Hydrate like you mean it.
  5. Home before you’re crispy — half-days are supposed to end with a grin, not a fuse.
Full-day · ~6–9 hours

Longer GNRT + wildlife side trip

Best for: people who want miles and sandhill cranes / wetlands energy.

  1. Morning paved miles — longer Great Northern out-and-back (full 8.5 miles each way if legs and battery agree). Start earlier in summer heat.
  2. Lunch & battery check — eat in Zimmerman or Elk River; top off the e-bike if you brought a charger for a long day.
  3. Sherburne NWR visitor areas — drive over toward County Road 1 / refuge access near Santiago. Park and explore on foot if bikes are restricted — that’s often the better wildlife experience anyway. Always check USFWS rules first.
  4. Optional soft stop — Oak Savanna Park / Sherburne History Center in Becker for a crushed-limestone stroll and overlook (verify surfaces; don’t force the e-bike where it doesn’t belong).
  5. Golden-hour glide home — lights ready if dusk is close; bug spray still in the bag.

What to pack

  • Water (more than you think)
  • Snacks that survive a jersey pocket
  • Spare tube / patch kit + pump or CO₂
  • Phone + offline map screenshot
  • Charger / power bank for long days
  • Layers (MN weather: spicy personality)
  • Bug spray (summer is not joking)
  • Lights (front + rear)
  • Helmet that actually fits
  • Small first-aid / blister kit
  • Cash/card for café victory lap
  • Patience for trail crossings & dogs

Seasonal reality check

🌱 Spring

Mud on soft surfaces; pavement still king. Layers for wind off the open prairie.

☀️ Summer

Bugs + heat. Early starts, extra water, sunscreen. Shade is treasure.

🍂 Fall

Ideal. Colors, cooler air, fewer mosquitoes. Watch leaf slick and earlier dark.

❄️ Winter

Ice/snow — fat/e-bike only where legal & safe. Check conditions; ego is not traction.

Distances are approximate. Battery range varies wildly with assist level, wind, temperature, and how many dad jokes you tell at rest stops.
